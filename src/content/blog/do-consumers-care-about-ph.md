A founder ships their product. DMs people on LinkedIn asking for upvotes. Gets #1 Product of the Day. 47 upvotes. 3 sign-ups. Traffic goes to zero the next day.

They screenshot the badge. "We did it."

I've watched this happen a lot. And the more I look at what actually happens on launch day, the more I think most founders are confused about what Product Hunt actually is.

## Who Actually Uses Product Hunt?

Other founders, builders, and people who work in tech. Not your customer.

If you're building a tool for accountants, HR teams, freelance designers, or basically anyone who doesn't spend their mornings browsing new launches, they are not on Product Hunt. They've probably never heard of it.

Go to r/SaaS and search for Product Hunt. You'll find the same thread posted every few months. One commenter put it pretty cleanly: "Focus on finding where your actual customers discover products. It's probably not Product Hunt."

## So Why Do Founders Keep Launching There?

Networking. That part is real.

If your product is a developer tool or a B2B SaaS where the buyer is a technical founder, Product Hunt is a reasonable channel. Your buyer is on there. The audience overlap actually works.

There's also the press angle. Some tech journalists check Product Hunt for story leads. That's a PR play, not a customer acquisition play, but it's real.

The problem isn't that founders use Product Hunt. The problem is what they think it's doing for them.

## The Upvote Problem Nobody Talks About

Before most launches, the founder posts in their Discord, DMs people on LinkedIn, and coordinates a wave of activity in the first few hours to push into the trending section.

It works. They hit the top 10.

And then they post that badge everywhere like 47 strangers validated their idea. But those weren't strangers. Those were other founders who clicked a button as a favor. No one evaluated the product. No one paid for anything. The ranking is manufactured, and everyone in the founder community knows it, but nobody says it out loud because they're all planning to do the same thing on their own launch day.

A #1 badge means other founders thought your launch page looked decent. That's it.

And it's not just Product Hunt. There are 250+ launch directories now. Same model, same audience. Just more platforms collecting the same badge from the same people.

## It Only Works If You Already Have Momentum

Basically, yes.

Products that get real traction almost always have something before the launch. A Twitter following, a newsletter, a community watching the build. The launch is an event for people who already know you, not a way to find people who don't.

The [playbook I use for zero-audience founders](/blog/idea-validation-playbook) cuts Product Hunt entirely until you have traction. It needs momentum to create momentum.

## When Does It Actually Make Sense?

If your buyer is a developer or technical founder who genuinely hangs out on Product Hunt, go for it. The overlap is real.

If you're optimizing for press and backlinks rather than direct signups, a good launch can get you coverage. That has SEO value.

If you've already built an audience, a coordinated launch gives your existing supporters something to rally around.

What doesn't work: launching cold, with no audience, expecting it to do your distribution. That's treating a networking tool like a marketing channel.

## My Take

I haven't launched Fewwords on Product Hunt yet. I'm not planning to until I have real users and real feedback to bring to it.

Product Hunt is a founder-to-founder community. It's good at what it is. The confusion happens when founders expect it to be something it isn't.

Real customers aren't refreshing Product Hunt on a Tuesday morning. They're in their inbox, on Reddit, in niche communities, googling their problem.

I have a prediction about where this ends up for indie founders. I'll write that piece separately. But launch platforms aren't dying. They're just getting more honest about who they're actually for.
